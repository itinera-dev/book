In Rust, `run` returns `Result<JourneyResult, Refusal>`, and the refusal is the `Err`. Its variants name what refused the journey, and each carries the error: `Refusal::WorkflowPolicy` when a workflow policy's `#[factory]` returns an `Err`, and `Refusal::DispatcherFactory` or `Refusal::Dispatcher` when the executor's dispatcher factory, or its dispatcher while the reporters are added, fails. The other causes of a refusal never reach `run` in Rust: a workflow put together wrongly is caught when it is built, by the compiler when it is declared with the attributes, or by `build` when it is declared by hand; a step, hook or reporter in a mode the executor does not accept does not compile; and initial data can only hold values, since its types must implement `Value`.
