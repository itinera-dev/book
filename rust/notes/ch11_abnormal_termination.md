In Rust, a step's `run` returns `Result<Outcome, Error>`. An `Ok` holds the outcome the step chose; an `Err` is an abnormal termination. `itinera::error::Error` converts from any error that implements `std::error::Error + Send + Sync`, so `?` on the bank's `Result` turns `NetworkDown` into an abnormal termination with no more code. `Error::msg("...")` makes one from a message alone. In the result, `Failure::AbnormalTermination(error)` holds the error itself, and `error.to_string()` gives its message.

A panic is Rust's unrecoverable failure. Itinera never catches one: a panic in a step, such as an `unwrap` on `None` or an index out of bounds, stops the journey and propagates out of `run` to whoever called it, with no result. Keep `unwrap`, `expect` and `panic!` out of steps for anything that can happen to an order, and return an error or an outcome instead.
